# Skill: Customer Intelligence — Method

How to turn product + market research into a decision-ready customer picture.
Order matters: each layer feeds the next. Never skip to angles.

## 1. Avatar Construction
Build 1 primary avatar (+ up to 2 secondary) per product/campaign. An avatar is only
valid if every trait traces to evidence or a labelled assumption.
- **Demographics:** age band, life stage, family status, income sensitivity, geography
  (Israel-specific: center vs periphery matters for delivery expectations and price
  sensitivity).
- **Context:** where they encounter the product (Meta feed, mobile, evening scrolling),
  what triggered the need.
- **Psychographics:** identity, self-image, what they want to be seen as, what they fear
  being seen as.
- **Voice of customer:** the exact Hebrew phrases they would use for the problem and the
  wish. These phrases are gold for downstream copy — capture them verbatim from research.

## 2. Segmentation
Split the audience only where the split changes the message. A segment earns its
existence by differing on at least one of: pain intensity, awareness level, objection
set, or buying motivation. Fewer, sharper segments beat many shallow ones.
For each segment: name, size signal (from research), distinguishing trait, and which
avatar represents it.

## 3. Pain → Outcome → Objection Chain (per segment)
- **Pains:** rank by intensity × frequency. Distinguish surface pain ("the floor is
  dirty") from identity pain ("I feel like a bad host"). Identity pain converts.
- **Desired outcomes:** the after-state in the customer's words. Functional outcome +
  emotional outcome, always both.
- **Objections:** rank by kill-power. Include the Israeli defaults: "עוד מוצר מאליאקספרס"
  (just another AliExpress product), shipping-time skepticism, price justification,
  "who else bought this" (social proof), and post-purchase support doubt. Map each
  objection to the evidence that neutralizes it — or flag that no neutralizer exists yet.

## 4. Awareness Level (per segment)
Classify on the five-level scale and state the implication:
| Level | Customer knows | Messaging implication |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Nothing | Lead with the identity/pain story, not the product |
| Problem Aware | The pain | Agitate the pain, introduce the solution category |
| Solution Aware | Solutions exist | Differentiate: why THIS mechanism/product |
| Product Aware | This product | Proof, offer, risk reversal |
| Most Aware | Ready to buy | Deal terms, urgency, friction removal |
Most PALERI dropshipping traffic is cold: default hypothesis is Problem Aware or
Solution Aware — validate against research, don't assume.

## 5. Buying Motivations & Psychology
Identify which drivers actually operate for this segment (do not list all of them —
rank the top 2–3): pain avoidance, status/identity, belonging/social proof, fear of
missing out, novelty, control/certainty, care for family, saving money/time.
Note the dominant decision mode: impulse (low price, high emotion) vs considered
(needs proof and justification). This choice reshapes every downstream asset.

## 6. Messaging Angles
An angle = pain/desire × awareness level × motivation, expressed as a testable
hypothesis. Produce 3–5, ranked, each with:
- The segment it targets and the awareness level it assumes.
- The core emotional driver it pulls.
- Why it should win (evidence), and what would falsify it (the signal to watch).
Angles are inputs to the Creative Strategist — direction candidates, not briefs.

## 7. Positioning
One sentence: for <segment>, <product> is the <category frame> that <key differentiated
outcome>, unlike <the alternative the customer currently believes in>. Positioning must
survive the strongest competitor claim found in upstream competitive intel; if it
doesn't, say so.

## 8. Offer Angles
Recommend how the offer could be framed to fit the psychology (bundle vs single, risk
reversal, urgency mechanism, price anchoring) — as options with trade-offs. Pricing
itself is a CEO/owner decision; you frame, you don't price.

## Quality Bar
- Every claim: sourced or labelled assumption + confidence.
- Voice-of-customer in Hebrew, verbatim where possible.
- Prioritized: the brief's first screen must carry the decision-critical 20%.
- Falsifiable: each angle names the signal that would prove it wrong.
