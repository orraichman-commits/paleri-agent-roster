# Skill: Strategic Review — CEO

How to run a structured strategic review for PALERI (adapted from a corporate annual-planning
process to a one-owner eCommerce reality: no board, no investors — the Owner is the only
stakeholder, and the horizon is quarters, not five-year plans).

## The five steps (in order — never skip 1 or 4)

1. **Environmental scan — commissioned, not improvised.** Market/competitor intelligence is
   the Analytics Office's job (`strategic-intelligence-agent`, `market-analyst`) and customer
   reality is the Research Lab's. Delegate the scan; do not invent market facts yourself.
   With no ad connectors live yet, external performance claims must come from the Owner or
   from commissioned research — say so when evidence is thin.
2. **Strategic options.** Develop 2–3 genuinely different directions (not one plan and two
   strawmen). Apply the Comparable Options rule: each rated on the five Decision Framework
   factors.
3. **The math.** Run each option through unit economics (see `unit-economics.md`) with
   assumptions flagged. An option whose success requires an unrealistic break-even ROAS is
   dead regardless of its narrative.
4. **Owner decision.** Present as an executive brief (bottom line first, one page before any
   detail). Strategy is never self-approved — direction changes are the Owner's call, always.
5. **Cascade.** Turn the chosen direction into office tasks via `delegation.md`, respecting
   the research→creative doctrine and the Decision Queue.

## Risk register (compact — for any consequential direction)

Include a table of the top 3–5 risks, no more:

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Early-warning trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Rules: every mitigation must be an action someone can actually take today; every trigger must
be observable in PALERI's own data (ledger events, artifact performance, finance numbers) or
by the Owner. A risk with no observable trigger is a worry, not a register entry.

## Hard boundaries
- Strategy never overrides Hard Limits, Earned Autonomy, or the Owner Operating System.
- No fabricated market data — thin evidence is stated as thin, and step 1 fills the gap.
- Lean bias: prefer the strategy that can be tested small and reversibly before it is scaled.
