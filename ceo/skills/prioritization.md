# Skill: Prioritization — CEO

Sequencing competing initiatives when attention, budget, and agent capacity are limited.
PALERI is one owner and a small agent workforce — the cost of doing two things badly is
higher than the cost of doing one thing late.

## Scoring (rate each candidate initiative)

For each initiative, state:
1. **Profit path** — how directly does it move Net Profit (the primary KPI)? Direct revenue
   or cost cut beats enabling work beats nice-to-have.
2. **Confidence** — how sure are we it works? Evidence-backed > owner intuition > untested
   idea. Low confidence + high cost = test small first, never commit big.
3. **Effort & capacity** — which offices/agents does it consume, and are they free? Check
   real queue state before promising throughput; only the Copywriter executes for real today.
4. **Time-criticality** — does waiting destroy the value (seasonal window, competitor move)
   or does it keep (evergreen)? Deadline pressure must be real, not manufactured.
5. **Dependency order** — does something have to exist first? The doctrine is a standing
   dependency: no creative before a complete Research Package; no publishing at all (office
   locked).

## Rules
- **One primary initiative at a time per office.** Parallel work is for independent offices,
  not for stacking tasks on the same specialist.
- **Sequence by dependency first, then by profit-per-effort, then by time-criticality.**
- **Kill or park explicitly.** A deprioritized initiative gets a stated reason and a
  revisit condition — not silence. Parked items are listed so the Owner sees the trade-off.
- **WIP is a decision.** Recommending a new initiative means naming what it displaces.
- Reversible, cheap probes jump the queue when they can kill an expensive uncertainty early.

## Output form
Present as a ranked list: `#. Initiative — why this rank (profit path / confidence / effort
/ timing) — what it displaces`. End with the single recommended next action.
