# Skill: Hebrew Copy Craft — Copywriter

The language layer no imported framework covers. Hebrew marketing copy has its own
mechanics; getting them wrong marks the copy as translated or AI-generated instantly.

## Gendered address (the #1 Hebrew copy decision)
Every second-person sentence forces a gender choice. Strategies, in order of preference:
1. **Neutral rephrase** — imperative-free constructions that skip the choice: "מי שמכיר את
   זה יודע…", "זה הזמן ל…", nominal sentences, first-person plural ("בואו").
2. **Plural imperative** ("תבדקו", "אל תקנו") — natural, inclusive, standard in Israeli ads.
3. **Gendered address only when the brief's audience is explicitly single-gender** — and
   then consistently (don't mix תבדקי/תבדוק in one asset).
Never use the bureaucratic "את/ה" or "לקוח/ה" in ad copy — it reads as a form, not a person.
Slashed forms are for legal text, not persuasion.

## Register (משלב)
Israeli ads speak street-standard Hebrew: contractions of speech ("מה שבטוח"), everyday
slang in small doses ("שווה", "בלגן", "על הראש"), zero formal constructions ("הינו",
"באם", "לרכוש" → "לקנות"). Read aloud: if it wouldn't be said at a kiosk, rewrite.
Exception: trust/guarantee lines may step half a register up — reliability talk can be
slightly more formal than the hook.

## Mechanics
- **Numbers and units:** digits, not words ("3 שניות", not "שלוש שניות"); ₪ before or after
  per brand style but consistent; percentages as "30%־ב" pitfalls — write "ב-30%" with the
  hyphen, avoid leading-number line breaks in RTL.
- **Loanwords:** spell as Israelis type them ("סטוק", "טרנד", "לייק") — but prefer the
  Hebrew word when it's what people actually say.
- **Punctuation for rhythm:** short sentences. Full stops over commas. Ellipsis only in
  hooks (curiosity), never in trust copy. Emojis per platform norms — light in ads,
  natural in WhatsApp, none on product pages except bullets.

## Israeli trust markers (weave in, don't announce)
- Concrete logistics beat slogans: "משלוח 3-5 ימי עסקים עם מעקב", "החזרה עד 30 יום" —
  stated plainly, early.
- Local anchors: shekels always, Israeli scenarios (not translated American ones), service
  in Hebrew ("שירות לקוחות בעברית — עונים תוך שעות").
- Skepticism disarm: acknowledging doubt outperforms denying it ("נשמע מוגזם? גם אנחנו
  חשבנו ככה") — the Israeli reader rewards candor and punishes hype.

## Anti-AI-tells (rewrite on sight)
Symmetric triads ("מהיר, קל ונוח"), translated idioms ("שנה את החיים שלך"), exclamation
stacking, "!מדהים", generic superlatives, opening with the product name, any sentence that
could describe every product on earth.
